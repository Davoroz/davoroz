Research

Empirical work on how prices move, how people perceive inflation, and how health systems can be measured and improved, built on large-scale data and careful econometrics.

Current agenda

My research brings microeconomic and macroeconomic questions to large, often unconventional data sources.

As a Master in Development Economics student at Dalhousie University, I am deepening my training in development economics. This builds on earlier work in three areas: price rigidities and online pricing dynamics measured with high-frequency data; the perception of inflation, measured through social media text; and health economics, using administrative and healthcare records to support policy-relevant analysis.

Across these projects, the common thread is method: building reliable data pipelines, applying econometric and machine-learning tools, and communicating results clearly to people who need to act on them.

Selected projects

Price rigidities and online pricing dynamics

Dalhousie University, Emerging Leaders in the Americas Program, 2024 to 2025

Research on how often and how sharply prices adjust, using high-frequency data and combining macroeconomic theory with statistical methods. The study of online pricing dynamics was supported by a Global Affairs Canada research scholarship for an academic stay in Canada.

  • High-frequency data
  • Macroeconomic theory
  • Statistical methods

Measuring inflation perceptions with social media

Central Bank of Colombia, Macroeconomic Modeling, 2022

Contributed to research on inflation expectations and perception, supporting the development of a novel indicator from social media data. I collected and processed more than 80 million tweets through the Twitter API and applied natural language processing and machine learning to classify the text and improve the measurement of inflation sentiment.

  • Python
  • Twitter API
  • NLP
  • Machine learning

Data and anomaly detection in health economics

ICESI University, Health Economics, 2022 to 2024

Applied research with large administrative and healthcare datasets. I built data pipelines and web-scraping tools in Python and R that automated the collection of more than 150,000 observations per month, and implemented machine-learning and statistical methods to detect anomalies in healthcare billing data worth roughly USD 1 million.

  • Python
  • R
  • Web scraping
  • Anomaly detection

Agricultural commodity prices and credit

Finagro, Economic Research, 2021 to 2022

Developed ARIMA, ARCH, and GARCH models to forecast agricultural commodity prices in support of credit and policy decisions, and studied the costs of originating agricultural credit to inform the evaluation of financial operations.

  • Time series
  • ARIMA / GARCH
  • R
